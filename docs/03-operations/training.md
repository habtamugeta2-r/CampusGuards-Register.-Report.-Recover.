# Training and communications

Technology pilots fail on behaviour, not on code. Two audiences: the officers who will hold the
phone, and the students who will decide whether to put a sticker on their laptop.

---

## 1. Officer training — two sessions, 45 minutes each

### Session 1: the system and the rules

1. **What this is for.** Recovering property. Not catching thieves, not monitoring students.
2. **The screen, live.** Each officer scans a real labelled device and sees each of the five
   outcomes. Practice the words they will say out loud for each.
3. **The five rules** (also on `/security/guidance`, and printed on the desk card):
   - A scan result is information, not a verdict.
   - Unregistered is not suspicious.
   - Check an ID only when you need to — and tick the box only when you did.
   - Scan for spot checks, not queues.
   - Someone carrying a reported device may be the person handing it in.
4. **What not to do.** No detention, no confiscation, no public searching, no accusation.
5. **Escalation.** Who is the supervisor on each shift, and how to reach them.

### Session 2: conditions and failure

1. **Offline.** Enable the cache, then turn the wifi off and scan: the result is labelled *cached*.
   Reconnect and watch the queue upload.
2. **Bad light, torn sticker, camera unavailable.** Practise image upload and manual entry until they
   are boring.
3. **Queue drill.** Two officers, a busy five minutes, and a stopwatch. If a queue forms, the answer
   is to stop scanning and tell the supervisor — not to scan faster.
4. **Data handling.** The device is shared; sign out at the end of the shift; report a lost handset
   immediately.

### Desk card (print, laminate, one per checkpoint)

```
CAMPUSGUARD — CHECKPOINT CARD

1. Scan the label (camera, photo, or type the code under it).
2. Read the screen aloud to the student if asked.
3. GREEN  → let them through.
   AMBER  → ask, don't accuse. Unregistered is not suspicious.
   RED    → "reported missing": do NOT detain or confiscate.
            Note the time, record the encounter, call your supervisor.
4. Check an ID only when you need to. Tick the box only if you did.
5. No queue. If one forms, stop scanning and call your supervisor.

Supervisor: ________________   Extension: ______
```

### Competency check

Before the pilot starts, each officer completes three consecutive scans unaided in under 20 seconds
and can state the four rules from memory. Record who passed, and when.

## 2. Student communications

**Tone:** honest, specific, and free of security-theatre language. Students have excellent detectors
for institutional overclaiming, and this product's entire value depends on their willingness to
participate voluntarily.

### What to say

- What CampusGuard is: a register that helps the university return devices that go missing.
- What it is not: it does not track your device, it does not prevent theft, and it does not give
  anyone your contact details.
- What is stored, and for how long: scan records for 90 days; reports anonymised after two years.
- What a checkpoint scan shows an officer: your device, your name and your student ID. Not your
  email, phone or serial number.
- That participation is voluntary and you can withdraw and delete your data at any time.
- That an unregistered device is not a suspicious device.

### Channels

| Channel | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| Departmental briefings with a live demo | Registration drive; the highest-conversion channel |
| Posters at the pilot location with a QR to the sign-up page | Passive recruitment |
| Student affairs email (short, plain) | Reach and legitimacy |
| A five-minute slot in orientation, if invited | Enrolment-time registration — the strongest lever for A1 |

### The registration drive

The pitch that works is not "help the university". It is:

> *If your laptop is taken, this is the difference between a shrug and a phone call saying we have
> it. One minute now, print the label, stick it on the underside.*

Then remove every obstacle: have labels and a printer at the desk, and a volunteer who does it with
them rather than pointing at a URL.

---

## 3. Ongoing

- **Weekly during the pilot:** a five-minute stand-up with the officers. Ask what annoyed them this
  week and write it down verbatim.
- **Monthly:** republish the registration rate to the departments taking part. A visible number
  creates gentle peer pressure.
- **At the end:** publish the results to participants before publishing them to anyone else —
  including the parts that did not work.
